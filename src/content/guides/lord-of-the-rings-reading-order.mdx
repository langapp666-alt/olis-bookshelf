Short version of the door: [Hobbit, Lord of the Rings, or Silmarillion first?](/questions/hobbit-or-silmarillion-first/). This page is the entry path.

The contested Tolkien question is not “which volume of *The Lord of the Rings* is first.” It is whether a new reader should begin in the First Age. Publication order and chronological order disagree, and they disagree on purpose. *The Silmarillion* was assembled and published after Tolkien’s death. It is a later door into earlier history.

This page is an **entry path**: *The Hobbit*, the three *Lord of the Rings* volumes, then *The Silmarillion* if you still want the deep back-shelf. It is not a Christopher Tolkien scholarly map, and it is not a film-order substitute.

Some printings bind the three *Lord of the Rings* volumes as one book. That is still the same novel in three parts. Do not treat a one-volume edition as a different work, and do not start in the middle because a shop sold *The Two Towers* as a standalone paperback.

The Jackson films are a cousin. Film order is close to this table for the main quest and is not a reason to skip *The Hobbit* book. The later Hobbit films are a different cousin.

**Contested order, labeled:** chronological charts put *The Silmarillion* (and sometimes *The Children of Húrin*) before *The Hobbit*. That is a reread or a lore project. For a first read it is the wrong temperature: a mythic summary written for people who already care why the later quest is expensive.

**House rule:** finish *The Hobbit* and *The Lord of the Rings*. If you still want the First Age, open *The Silmarillion*. If you bounce off the first chapters, that is information, not a moral failing. Come back later. Do not “prepare” for Frodo by reading the Ainulindalë first.

*The History of Middle-earth* and *The Nature of Middle-earth* stay off this table. They are working papers, not a first-read series.

*The Adventures of Tom Bombadil* and the LotR appendices are extras. The appendices sit at the end of *The Return of the King*. Read them after the novel if you want the timelines. They are not book one.

## Common mistakes

- Starting with *The Silmarillion* because it is “earlier.”
- Starting with *The Fellowship of the Ring* and treating *The Hobbit* as optional children’s filler.
- Opening *The Children of Húrin* as a gentle on-ramp.
- Using the Hobbit films as a table of contents for the novel.
- Waiting for a “complete chronological box set” before reading *The Hobbit*.

## What to read next

1. **[The Wheel of Time](/guides/wheel-of-time-reading-order/) (Robert Jordan)** — If you want another long quest with a prequel that should stay a prequel.
2. **[Earthsea](/guides/earthsea-reading-order/) (Ursula K. Le Guin)** — If you want a shorter archipelago with its own contested later books, not a Second Age syllabus.
3. **[Kingkiller Chronicle](/guides/kingkiller-chronicle-reading-order/) (Patrick Rothfuss)** — If you want another voice-forward epic that starts at book one even while book three stays unpublished. Start at *The Name of the Wind*.
4. **[The Stormlight Archive](/guides/stormlight-archive-reading-order/) (Brandon Sanderson)** — If you want a modern long epic and a first book that is already a door, not a footnote.
5. **The Silmarillion, on a second visit.** If *The Return of the King* left you hungry for the older wars, not for a different author’s series.

## FAQ

**Can I start with The Lord of the Rings?**  
You can. You will miss the tone *Fellowship* is in conversation with. *The Hobbit* is short. Read it first.

**Should I read The Silmarillion before The Hobbit?**  
Not for a first read. That is the main contested order this page exists to label.

**Is The Hobbit required?**  
For this house, yes. It is the published on-ramp.

**Which Lord of the Rings edition?**  
Any complete text. One volume or three. Avoid starting on a “Part Two” paperback.

**Do I need Unfinished Tales?**  
No. It is a later shelf.
