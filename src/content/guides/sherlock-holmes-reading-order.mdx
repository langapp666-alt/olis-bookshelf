Short version of the door: [Where to start Sherlock Holmes](/questions/where-to-start-sherlock-holmes/). This page is the nine-book canon.

The Doyle canon is **four novels and fifty-six short stories** gathered into five collections. That is the finished set. Publication order of those nine books is the first-read path.

Chronological reshuffles exist because *The Hound of the Baskervilles* and *The Valley of Fear* are set earlier than their publication dates. They were still issued to people who already knew Holmes. This house keeps them where they were published.

**House rule:** read 1–4, then *Hound*, then the return. If you only want one novel after the meeting, *Hound* is the usual second visit — after you already know who Holmes is. It is not book one.

You do not need to read the shorts inside a collection in magazine-date order. The collection is the unit.

## The “chronological” trap

Holmesians keep a career calendar. It is a hobby, not a first-read tool. *Hound* is set before Reichenbach and published after *Memoirs*. *Valley of Fear* sits in the middle of the life and at the end of the novel list. Publication order is how contemporary readers met those books. Chronology is a later argument.

## Pastiches and screen orders — optional later

Anything not by Doyle is **not** the canon. That includes plays Doyle touched, apocrypha, and the modern novels that borrow the name.

## Common mistakes

- Starting with *Hound* because it is the novel people remember.
- Starting with *The House of Silk* or a “complete Sherlock” omnibus that leads with a pastiche.
- Reading *Memoirs* last because “last” is in the title of a different collection.
- Using a timeline spreadsheet before *A Study in Scarlet*.

## FAQ

**Do I have to read every short?**  
For “the canon,” yes, eventually. For a first visit, *Study*, *Adventures*, and *Hound* are a complete introduction.

**Is Hound a prequel?**  
It is set earlier than its publication. Read it fifth, or after you already know Holmes. Do not read it first.

**Which collection spelling?**  
*The Case-Book* (UK 1927) is the same book as *The Case Book*.

**Are the apocryphal Doyle pieces required?**  
No.
