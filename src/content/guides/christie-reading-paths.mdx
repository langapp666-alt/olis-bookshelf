  Styles (1920) is the first Poirot novel. *The Murder at the Vicarage* (1930) is the first Marple novel. Pick **one** detective and stay there for a while. Do not start with *Curtain* or *Sleeping Murder* because they were held back and published last. Do not start with *And Then There Were None* and call that Poirot 1 — it is a standalone.
</StartHere>

Christie is not one series. Retailers stack fifty titles as if *The Body in the Library* were *Styles* 12. This page is two **paths**: the Poirot novels in publication order, and the Marple novels in publication order. Short-story collections stay labeled. Famous standalones stay off both ladders.

The official Christie site publishes separate Poirot and Marple reading lists. We follow those doors. We do not invent a single “complete Christie” spreadsheet.

## Path A — Hercule Poirot (novels)

Publication order is the first read. The estate’s own list says you *can* hop; *Curtain* is still last. Hastings, the detective-fiction jokes, and Poirot’s age are cheaper if you start at Styles.

**House rule:** you do not owe all thirty-three. A first visit can stop after five. If you want the life of the detective, *Curtain* stays last.

Poirot shorts (*Poirot Investigates*, *Murder in the Mews*, *The Labours of Hercules*, and later gatherings) are optional. They are not secret novels sitting between these rows.

## Path B — Miss Marple (novels)

Twelve novels. The first Marple *appearance* is in shorts later collected as *The Thirteen Problems* (1932). The first Marple *novel* is still *The Murder at the Vicarage*. For a first read, start there.

*The Thirteen Problems* (also *The Tuesday Night Club*) is the short-story door. Read it after Vicarage, or as a side plate. It is not a required prequel.

*Nemesis* assumes you already met certain people in *A Caribbean Mystery*. That pair is the one Marple sequence you should not shuffle.

## Common mistakes

- Starting with *Murder on the Orient Express* or *Death on the Nile* because a film is on.
- Starting with *Curtain* or *Sleeping Murder* because they look like finales and therefore “important.”
- Treating *And Then There Were None* as Poirot.
- Making one publication list of every Christie title and calling it a series.

## FAQ

**Can I start with Orient Express?**  
You can follow that case. You will not have watched Poirot become expensive. Start with *Styles* if you want the path.

**Do I need every Poirot short?**  
No.

**Is Sleeping Murder a prequel?**  
It was written earlier and published last. Read it last among the Marple novels.

**Which path if I only want one famous title?**  
*And Then There Were None* is the honest one-book Christie. It is not a series door.
