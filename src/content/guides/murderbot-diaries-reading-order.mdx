## Starting point

Start with **All Systems Red**. It is the first published story and the first story in which this SecUnit is working a survey contract under a name it gave itself. You do not need the shorts first. You do not need the later novels to understand the novella.

If you already watched a screen adaptation, still start here. Adaptations compress; the reading-order problem on this shelf is about novellas vs novels and one book that was published out of timeline order.

## Chronological exception

**Fugitive Telemetry is set before Network Effect.** The author’s page says so in those words. If you want events in story time, read it after Exit Strategy (and after the optional short “Home”) and before Network Effect.

We still recommend **publication order on a first pass** for two reasons:

1. That is how most printings and library holds will hand the books to you.
2. Network Effect was written and published as the jump to novel length. Inserting the later novella mid-binge is fine; treating chronology as a purity test is not.

If you are rereading, chronological order is reasonable.

### Flag: Rapport

Rapport is the short we will not quietly fold into the numbered list.

- It is a web short, not a numbered diary volume.
- It is from ART’s point of view. That is a different camera than the diaries.
- Fan placement puts it after Artificial Condition and well before Network Effect. That is a chronology guess, not a publisher spine number.
- Some readers will prefer to wait until they have already met ART in Artificial Condition *and* spent a novel with ART. Others will want it in timeline order. We will not pretend there is one correct piety.

Human review should confirm whether a 2026 collected edition has made this less optional.

## Common mistakes

- **Waiting for a “real novel” before starting.** All Systems Red is the start. The first four books are novellas on purpose.
- **Assuming publication order is chronological after Exit Strategy.** Fugitive Telemetry is the book that breaks that assumption.
- **Reading Compulsory first because it is “earlier.”** It is earlier. It is also a fragment. It will not teach you the series.
- **Treating the TV show as a substitute table of contents.** Use the author’s list.

## What to read next

These are not rankings. Each one is a reason.

1. **A Closed and Common Orbit (Becky Chambers)** — If the draw was personhood after leaving a constrained role, Chambers spends a whole novel on the aftercare that Murderbot keeps side-eyeing.
2. **Ancillary Justice (Ann Leckie)** — If you want a ship-mind and a body problem that is colder and more imperial than Preservation politics.
3. **Service Model (Adrian Tchaikovsky)** — If the humor is the point: a robot trying to do the job correctly in a world that has stopped making sense.
4. **The Expanse (James S. A. Corey)** — If you finished the corporate-survey scale and want crew politics at system scale. Our [Expanse guide](/guides/the-expanse-reading-order/) is the next shelf over.
5. **The Locked Tomb (Tamsyn Muir)** — If the voice is the addiction: sarcasm as armor, grief as plot, and a refusal to explain the joke until book two.

## FAQ

**Do I need the shorts?**  
No. Read them if you already like the voice and want extra angles.

**Can I skip Fugitive Telemetry?**  
You can. You will miss a station murder investigation that sits between the first arc and the novel-length books. You will not make Network Effect unreadable.

**Is Platform Decay a starting point?**  
No. It is a continuation after System Collapse.

**Which collected edition should I buy?**  
Buy whatever your library or bookstore has for All Systems Red. Collections are a format choice, not a reading-order choice.
