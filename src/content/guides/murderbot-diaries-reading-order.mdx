## Publication order

This is the first-read path. Titles and years follow Martha Wells’ series page for the books it lists, with Platform Decay added from the current Tor/Macmillan listing.

Exit Strategy closes the first arc. Network Effect is the jump to novel length. Fugitive Telemetry arrived after that jump even though it is set earlier.

A special hardcover collecting the first four novellas plus the short “Home: Habitat, Range, Niche, Territory” exists. Treat that as a convenience binding, not a ninth main book.

Tor catalogs **Platform Decay** as Murderbot Diaries Volume 8, on sale 2026-05-05, 256 pages. That is a main-sequence volume, not a skippable extra. It is shorter than *Network Effect*. Do not wait for a “real novel” after *System Collapse*. Wells’ own series page, when last opened, still listed only *Network Effect* and *System Collapse* under Novels. The publisher listing is ahead of the author page.

## Chronological exception

**Fugitive Telemetry is set before Network Effect.** Wells’ page says so in those words. If you want events in story time, read it after Exit Strategy (and after the optional short “Home”) and before Network Effect.

We still recommend **publication order on a first pass**:

1. That is how most printings and library holds will hand the books to you.
2. Network Effect was written and published as the jump to novel length. Inserting the later novella mid-binge is fine; treating chronology as a purity test is not.

If you are rereading, chronological order is reasonable.

Rapport is a Reactor novelette (10 July 2025), told from ART’s point of view. Reactor’s introduction says it can be read after *Artificial Condition*. Some bibliographic notes warn that later-book furniture shows up in the ART-crew names. If you are spoiler-sensitive about who ART works with after the early novellas, wait until after *Network Effect*.

**House rule:** do not number it. Do not start with it. Do not treat it as homework between books 2 and 3.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting for a “real novel” before starting. All Systems Red is the start. The first four books are novellas on purpose.
- Assuming publication order is chronological after Exit Strategy. Fugitive Telemetry is the book that breaks that assumption.
- Reading Compulsory first because it is “earlier.” It is earlier. It is also a fragment.
- Sliding Rapport into the numbered column.
- Treating the TV show as a table of contents.

## What to read next

1. **A Closed and Common Orbit (Becky Chambers)** — If the draw was personhood after leaving a constrained role, Chambers spends a whole novel on the aftercare that Murderbot keeps side-eyeing.
2. **Ancillary Justice (Ann Leckie)** — If you want a ship-mind and a body problem that is colder and more imperial than Preservation politics.
3. **Service Model (Adrian Tchaikovsky)** — If the humor is the point: a robot trying to do the job correctly in a world that has stopped making sense.
4. **[The Expanse](/guides/the-expanse-reading-order/) (James S. A. Corey)** — If you finished the corporate-survey scale and want crew politics at system scale.
5. **[The Locked Tomb](/guides/the-locked-tomb-reading-order/) (Tamsyn Muir)** — If the voice is the addiction: sarcasm as armor, grief as plot, and a refusal to explain the joke until book two.

## FAQ

**Do I need the shorts?**  
No. Read them if you already like the voice and want extra angles.

**Can I skip Fugitive Telemetry?**  
You can. You will miss a station murder investigation that sits between the first arc and the novel-length books. You will not make Network Effect unreadable.

**Is Platform Decay a starting point?**  
No. It is Volume 8, after System Collapse.

**Where does Rapport go?**  
Optional. After Artificial Condition if you follow Reactor’s note; after Network Effect if you want fewer later-book names. Never first.

**Which collected edition should I buy?**  
Buy whatever your library or bookstore has for All Systems Red. Collections are a format choice, not a reading-order choice.
