## Starting point

Start with **Leviathan Wakes**. It is book one of nine. Do not start with a novella. Do not start with *Memory’s Legion*. Do not start with a prequel because someone online said “chronological is more complete.”

James S. A. Corey is Daniel Abraham and Ty Franck. The novels are the spine. The shorter fiction is connective tissue, and several pieces are *earlier in time* than the book that makes them safe.

## Memory’s Legion

**Memory’s Legion** (hardcover 2022; Orbit trade paperback 2023) is labeled “The Complete Expanse Story Collection.” The publisher page lists eight pieces, in this print order:

1. Drive
2. The Butcher of Anderson Station
3. The Churn
4. Gods of Risk
5. The Vital Abyss
6. Strange Dogs
7. Auberon
8. The Sins of Our Fathers

Use the book as a one-volume cleanup after *Leviathan Falls*, or as a way to catch novellas you skipped, in the order the collection prints them.

Do not use it as book one. A collection that includes prequels and a coda is a toolbox, not a prologue.

## Chronological prequels spoil

Several shorts take place *before* or *alongside* early novels. That does not make them on-ramps.

- **The Churn** is an Amos-beforehand story. Meeting Amos in the novels first is the point.
- **The Butcher of Anderson Station** fills in a past that the novels already know how to withhold.
- **The Vital Abyss** runs across years that include events the early novels treat as discoveries.
- **Drive** is historically early and still a poor first impression of the series voice.

If a chart on the internet says “true chronological order,” treat it as a reread toy. **First read: publication order.**

### The Vital Abyss — do not read the marketing
## The Vital Abyss

Some official and storefront descriptions tell you whose custody you are in, which catastrophe you are circling, and what kind of research the narrators were doing. That is mid-series furniture. A reader who is still in the first two novels should not meet that furniture in a blurb.

**House rule:** give the title, the year, and “read it in publication sequence, not as a prequel appetizer.” If you need to warn someone, warn them that *the marketing text* is the spoiler, then stop.

## Optional arcs

- **Novels only.** Completely valid. You will understand the nine-book argument.
- **Novels, then Memory’s Legion.** Best cleanup if you do not want to buy novellas one at a time.
- **Publication order with novellas interleaved.** Best if you like the original drip of side stories.
- **TV first, then novels.** The show diverges. Do not use season order as a book order.

## Common mistakes

- Starting with *The Churn* or *Drive* “because they are first.”
- Reading *Memory’s Legion* before *Leviathan Wakes*.
- Treating *The Last Flight of the Cassandra* as book 8.5.
- Trusting a *Vital Abyss* storefront paragraph.
- Reading *The Vital Abyss* before *Cibola Burn* because a timeline chart said so.

## FAQ

**Do I have to read the novellas?**  
No. They add character history and a few bridges. The nine novels close the series.

**Is the show a substitute?**  
No. It is a cousin. Characters and late-plot choices diverge.

**Where does Memory’s Legion go?**  
After *Leviathan Falls*, unless you are interleaving shorts as they were published.

**Is The Last Flight of the Cassandra in Memory’s Legion?**  
Not on the Orbit contents list we opened. Treat it as an RPG extra.
