James S. A. Corey is Daniel Abraham and Ty Franck. The novels are the spine. The shorter fiction is connective tissue, and several pieces are *earlier in time* than the book that makes them safe.

## Memory’s Legion

**Memory’s Legion** (hardcover 2022; Orbit trade paperback 2023) is labeled “The Complete Expanse Story Collection.” The publisher page lists eight pieces, in this print order: Drive; The Butcher of Anderson Station; The Churn; Gods of Risk; The Vital Abyss; Strange Dogs; Auberon; The Sins of Our Fathers.

Use the book as a one-volume cleanup after *Leviathan Falls*, or as a way to catch novellas you skipped, in the order the collection prints them. Do not use it as book one.

## Chronological prequels spoil

Several shorts take place *before* or *alongside* early novels. That does not make them on-ramps.

- **The Churn** is an Amos-beforehand story. Meeting Amos in the novels first is the point.
- **The Butcher of Anderson Station** fills in a past that the novels already know how to withhold.
- **The Vital Abyss** runs across years that include events the early novels treat as discoveries. Some official and storefront descriptions tell you whose custody you are in. That is mid-series furniture. We are not quoting those blurbs.
- **Drive** is historically early and still a poor first impression of the series voice.

If a chart on the internet says “true chronological order,” treat it as a reread toy. **First read: publication order.**

## Common mistakes

- Starting with *The Churn* or *Drive* “because they are first.”
- Reading *Memory’s Legion* before *Leviathan Wakes*.
- Treating *The Last Flight of the Cassandra* as book 8.5.
- Trusting a *Vital Abyss* storefront paragraph.
- Reading *The Vital Abyss* before *Cibola Burn* because a timeline chart said so.

## What to read next

1. **[The Murderbot Diaries](/guides/murderbot-diaries-reading-order/) (Martha Wells)** — If you want a smaller camera after nine books of system politics: one SecUnit, contracts, and media as coping.
2. **[Red Rising](/guides/red-rising-reading-order/) (Pierce Brown)** — If you want another solar-system class war, with the warning that book four is a second argument, not a victory lap. Short door: [where to start Red Rising](/questions/where-to-start-red-rising/).
3. **[Dune](/guides/dune-novels-reading-order/) (Frank Herbert)** — If the politics-and-ecology scale was the hook and you want the older monument, with a hard stop after Herbert’s six.
4. **The Mercy of Gods (James S. A. Corey)** — If you want the same writers, a new setting, and no homework from the Rocinante.
5. **[The Culture](/guides/the-culture-reading-order/) (Iain M. Banks)** — If you want post-scarcity machines and the moral hangover of intervention, without treating *Consider Phlebas* as a mandatory bruise.

## FAQ

**Do I have to read the novellas?**  
No. They add character history and a few bridges. The nine novels close the series.

**Is the show a substitute?**  
No. It is a cousin. Characters and late-plot choices diverge.

**Where does Memory’s Legion go?**  
After *Leviathan Falls*, unless you are interleaving shorts as they were published.

**Is The Last Flight of the Cassandra in Memory’s Legion?**  
Not on the Orbit contents list we opened. Treat it as an RPG extra.
