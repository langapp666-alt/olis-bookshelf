The contested Foundation question is publication versus in-universe time. The 1951 novel is the first published Foundation book, collecting earlier stories. *Prelude to Foundation* and *Forward the Foundation* were written for people who already knew the original arc. The Apple TV+ series is a cousin, not book one.

Short version of the order question: [Foundation: publication or chronological?](/questions/foundation-publication-or-chronological/). Same “do not start with the prequel” argument on Arrakis: [Should I read the Brian Herbert Dune books?](/questions/should-i-read-brian-herbert-dune/). This page is the full path.

**House rule:** original trilogy, then *Edge* and *Earth* if you want the 1980s continuation, then the prequels if you still want Hari’s earlier life. Chronological-first is a reread.

The wider “Asimov universe” charts that require *I, Robot* and the Empire novels before *Foundation* are optional connective tissue. They are not a Foundation on-ramp. *I, Robot* is a different door you can open later.

## Common mistakes

- Starting with *Prelude* because it is “first.”
- Using the TV show as a table of contents.
- Treating *Foundation and Earth* as optional filler you read first because of a cover.
- Waiting for a new streaming season before opening 1951.

## FAQ

**Where to start Foundation?**  
*Foundation* (1951). It is the first published Foundation novel.

**Foundation publication or chronological?**  
Publication first. Prequels later, or not at all, on a first pass.

**Should I read Prelude to Foundation first?**  
No. It is later-published earlier time, written for people who already knew the original trilogy.

**Do I need I, Robot or the Empire novels first?**  
No. Those are other Asimov doors. They are not a Foundation on-ramp.

**Is the Apple TV+ show a substitute?**  
No. It is a cousin. It is not a license to start with a prequel novel.
