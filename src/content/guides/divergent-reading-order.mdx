A finished trilogy, then a companion volume, then a short epilogue. Publication order is the first-read path. Chronology inside *Four* runs earlier and then beside book one. That is why it is a later book.

Roth has said, in so many words, to read the published trilogy first and save *Four* until after. The companion assumes you already know the factions and what they cost.

**Contested order, labeled:** chronological charts put *The Transfer*, *The Initiate*, and *The Son* before *Divergent*, then *The Traitor* beside it. Roth’s own advice is the opposite of that chart for a first read. *Four* does not rebuild the world. It assumes you already live there.

**House rule:**

1. *Divergent*
2. *Insurgent*
3. *Allegiant*
4. *Four* if you want Tobias’s earlier rooms
5. *We Can Be Mended* if you want the short afterword

*Carve the Mark* is a different Roth series. It is not Divergent 6.

## Common mistakes

- Starting with *Four* because the stories are “earlier.”
- Starting with *Allegiant* because that is the argument people remember from the cinema.
- Treating *We Can Be Mended* as a required fifth novel.
- Searching “Divergent” and picking up a film tie-in or companion as book one.

## FAQ

**Do I need Four?**  
No. The trilogy ends.

**Should I read Four between Divergent and Insurgent?**  
Not for a first read. Roth prefers the published trilogy first.

**Is We Can Be Mended a novel?**  
No. It is a short epilogue.

**Movie order?**  
The first two films follow the books more closely than the third. None of them is a reason to start at *Four*.
