This page is **Frank Herbert’s six novels**, then a labeled optional pair written later by Brian Herbert and Kevin J. Anderson. You can stop after book one and have read *Dune*. You can stop after *Chapterhouse: Dune* and have read the series Herbert kept writing. The expanded line — Houses, Jihad, Schools, Caladan — is a different, much larger project, not a hidden book one.

Short version of the door: [Where to start Dune](/questions/where-to-start-dune/). Stop/continue after the six: [Should I read the Brian Herbert Dune books?](/questions/should-i-read-brian-herbert-dune/). Same publication-first argument at Trantor: [Foundation: publication or chronological?](/questions/foundation-publication-or-chronological/).

Herbert’s six are complete as a published argument. *Chapterhouse: Dune* ends on a hinge. Whether you walk through that hinge is a separate decision.

## Frank Herbert’s six

Publication order is the only honest first read. There is no contested sequence inside these six.

You can stop after book one. You will have read *Dune*. You will not have read the series Herbert kept writing, which immediately argues with the ending people remember from the films.

The Villeneuve films cover *Dune* and then *Dune Messiah*. They are cousins. Film part two is not a license to skip the 1965 novel, and it is not a substitute for books 3–6.

## The later finale pair (optional, labeled)

After Herbert’s death, Brian Herbert and Kevin J. Anderson published two novels presented as a conclusion drawn from notes: *Hunters of Dune* (2006) and *Sandworms of Dune* (2007). Tor still frames them that way.

This is a contested continuation. We are not pretending otherwise.

**House rule:** finish Herbert’s six first. If you want a published closing of the *Chapterhouse* hinge, these two are the books that attempt it. If you want Herbert’s voice and you are willing to leave a hinge as a hinge, stop at book 6. Either is honest. Starting the series with *Hunters* is not.

## Prequels are a different shelf

The expanded line is large: *House Atreides* and the rest of Prelude to Dune; Legends of Dune / the Butlerian Jihad; Heroes of Dune; Great Schools; the Caladan trilogy; *Princess of Dune*; and more. Those books are earlier in the calendar and later in publishing history. They also change the temperature of the original six.

**House rule:** do not read them first. Do not read them as required homework between *Dune* and *Dune Messiah*. If you finish Herbert and still want more Arrakis, you can enter that catalog knowing it is a sequel industry, not a hidden book one.

We are not listing every expanded-universe title here. Those lists go stale and they crowd out the six novels people actually searched for.

## Common mistakes

- Starting with *House Atreides* or the Jihad trilogy because a chronological chart said so.
- Skipping *Dune* because you watched the films.
- Stopping after the films’ coverage and calling the series finished when you meant “I read one and a half stories.”
- Treating *Hunters of Dune* as Frank Herbert book 7 without noticing the byline.
- Waiting for another film before opening the 1965 novel.

## FAQ

**Dune books in order — where do I start?**  
*Dune* (1965). Then Herbert’s remaining five if you want the series he kept writing.

**Should I only read Frank Herbert?**  
For a first read, yes: the six. Brian Herbert books are optional later. Jihad and House prequels wait.

**Should I read the Brian Herbert Dune books?**  
Not first. Finish Herbert’s six. *Hunters* and *Sandworms* are an optional later finale. Short version: [Should I read the Brian Herbert Dune books?](/questions/should-i-read-brian-herbert-dune/).

**Should I read the prequels first for “background”?**  
No. They are later books about earlier time.

**Can I start with the movies?**  
You can watch first. Start the books at *Dune*. Film part two is not a skip-code for the 1965 novel.

**Is there a Dune 7 by Frank Herbert?**  
Not as a finished novel. *Hunters* and *Sandworms* are the later attempt.

**Are the Brian Herbert books canon?**  
They are published sequels/prequels in the same setting. They are not Frank Herbert’s six. Readers disagree; this page labels them instead of baptizing them.
