  It is book one of a finished four-book climb. There is no prequel, no side-novel on-ramp, and no honest way to begin with *Arm of the Sphinx* because the first book “is slow.”
</StartHere>

The first book *is* deliberate. Senlin is a headmaster with a guidebook. The Tower trains him out of that. Starting at book two hands you a crew and a problem you have not earned.

Some readers bounce. That is a taste problem, not a reading-order problem. The series does not have a gentler door.

The 2018 Orbit editions are the same first two books, not a reboot. If your library has the later covers, you are still in the same sequence.

Bancroft’s series page lists them as Book I–IV of the tetralogy. There is no contested chronology. People who “argue Senlin vs sequels as a start” are arguing about patience, not about hidden prequels.

## Optional extras

This is a short spine. We are not stuffing picture books or RPG extras into it.

If you find a later anthology or related short, read it after the tetralogy. Nothing currently belongs in a numbered fifth slot. Do not wait for a fifth novel before starting; *The Fall of Babel* is the end of this climb.

## Common mistakes

- Starting with *Arm of the Sphinx* because a review said the series finds its crew in book two.
- Treating the Orbit reprints as Books 1–2 of a different series.
- Searching “Babel” and picking up a different author’s tower.
- Stopping after book one and calling the series unfinished. It was unfinished in 2013. It is not unfinished now.

## FAQ

**Do I have to read them in order?**  
Yes. It is one climb.

**Is Senlin Ascends optional if I already know the premise?**  
No. The premise is not the book.

**Which edition should I buy?**  
Whichever printing your shop has. Orbit 2018 is the widely available first volume, not a rewrite.

**Is there a book five?**  
Not as part of this tetralogy. *The Fall of Babel* is the last novel on the author’s series page.

**Is this related to R. F. Kuang’s Babel?**  
No. Different book, different argument. Search “Senlin” if the title is crowded.
