## Where to stop, honestly

Frank Herbert’s six novels are a complete published argument. You can stop after *Dune* and have read a novel. You can stop after *Chapterhouse: Dune* and have read the series Herbert kept writing. *Chapterhouse* ends on a hinge. Leaving a hinge as a hinge is allowed.

The Villeneuve films cover *Dune* and then *Dune Messiah*. They are cousins. They are not a license to skip the 1965 novel, and they are not a substitute for books 3–6.

## The later finale pair

After Herbert’s death, Brian Herbert and Kevin J. Anderson published *Hunters of Dune* and *Sandworms of Dune*, framed as a conclusion from notes. They are a contested continuation. This house labels them instead of baptizing them.

House rule: Herbert’s six first. Then either walk through the hinge with those two, or do not. Starting the series with *Hunters* is not a path.

## Prequels are a different shelf

Prelude to Dune, the Jihad/Legends books, Schools, Caladan, and the rest are earlier in the calendar and later in publishing history. They change the temperature of the original six. Do not read them first. Do not read them as homework between *Dune* and *Dune Messiah*.

## FAQ

**Dune books in order — where do I start?**  
*Dune* (1965). Then Herbert’s remaining five if you want the series he kept writing.

**Should I only read Frank Herbert?**  
For a first read, yes: the six. Brian Herbert books are optional later.

**Are the Brian Herbert books canon?**  
They are published sequels and prequels in the same setting. They are not Frank Herbert’s six. Readers disagree.

**Should I read the prequels for background?**  
No. They are later books about earlier time.

**Is there a Dune 7 by Frank Herbert?**  
Not as a finished novel. *Hunters* and *Sandworms* are the later attempt.
